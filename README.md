# Markov Chain Monte Carlo on SO(n) via Representation Theory

A paper on the spectral gap of the Zappa–Holmes-Cerfon–Goodman manifold sampler on the rotation
group, together with a Lean 4 formalization of every numbered statement in it except the last
theorem and its corollary (the exact spectral gap on SO(n) for every n), whose proofs use the
Peter–Weyl theorem; the two lemmas that carry their computations are formalized.

**Status: AI-generated, not peer reviewed.** The paper, its proofs, the Lean development and the
numerics were generated by two of Anthropic's Claude models (Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Opus
5.5), run as agents in Claude Code over several sessions directed by the first author. The starting point was the abstract of an earlier project
by the two authors, which treated D_n and SO(2) and left SO(n) as ongoing work. What that means
for each result:

- Everything in the table under "What is proved" is checked by the Lean kernel.
- Theorem 7.5 and Corollary 7.6 (the exact gap for every n) are paper proofs. They were written
  by one of the two models (Opus 5.5) and reviewed by the other (Fable 5.2), and tested
  numerically, which is weaker evidence than a formal proof or a referee's report.
- Whether the results are new has not been established. Searches by the same models found no
  earlier determination of this sampler's spectral gap on SO(n); that is not a specialist's
  judgement.

## Proved on paper, not formalized

| Result | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Thm 7.5** | **SO(n), every n ≥ 2: if the step distribution is p·δ₁ plus a multiple of an O(n)-conjugation-invariant Lipschitz density that is nonincreasing in each rotation angle on [0, π], then the spectral gap is exactly 1 − E[tr R]/n and E[tr R]/n is the L² contraction coefficient.** |
| **Cor 7.6** | **The Gaussian sampler on SO(n) has such a step distribution, so its spectral gap is exactly 1 − E[tr R]/n for every n ≥ 2 and σ ≥ 0.** Earlier versions of the paper left the case n ≥ 4 open. |

The proof integrates by parts on SO(n): for a matrix coefficient h with Laplacian eigenvalue
−c, ∫ h dν = c⁻¹ ∫ ⟨∇f, ∇h⟩, where f is the density of ν. Averaged over conjugation and
restricted to the maximal torus, h becomes a nonnegative combination of products of cosines, and
Lemma 7.3 shows that its gradient divided by c is dominated by the gradient of tr R / n divided
by n − 1. Integrating against the gradient of a density that decreases with the angles gives
|λ| ≤ E[tr R]/n for every nontrivial representation. The steps around Lemmas 7.3 and 7.4 use
standard facts that are not in Mathlib and are not formalized here: the Peter–Weyl theorem and
Schur's lemma for SO(n), Rademacher's theorem, and the change of variables formula on SO(n) with
Haar measure as Riemannian volume (paper, Remark 7.7). One further ingredient, the normal form of
rotations (every R in SO(n) is P t(θ) Pᵀ with P in SO(n)), is formalized in
`lean/SOnMCMC/NormalForm.lean` (`exists_torusMat_conj`) as the first step of
`lean/ROADMAP-SOnGap.md`; no numbered statement of the paper cites it yet.

Both results are tested numerically in `numerics/check.py` (sections 5, 7 and 8): Theorem 7.5 on
96 angle-monotone densities other than the Gaussian one for n = 4, …, 7, and the density formula
of Corollary 7.6 against simulation for n = 3, …, 6.

## What is not machine-checked

- **Theorem 7.5 and Corollary 7.6 are not formalized** (see the previous section). For n = 2 and
  n = 3 the exact gap of the Gaussian sampler is also machine-checked by the earlier, separate
  routes (Theorems 4.10, 5.16 and Corollary 6.12).
- **The relation to a particular implementation is a modelling step.** The chain
  X ↦ X·R(A) is constructed and analysed in Lean, including the projection, the reverse check,
  the Metropolis–Hastings test and the Newton iteration with frozen Jacobian. Not formalized: the
  full Newton iteration with an updated Jacobian, finite stopping rules and floating-point
  arithmetic (Remark 5.5). `numerics/check.py` checks the solver claim empirically.
- Remarks marked "(not formalized)" in the paper: the classical Peter–Weyl picture for SO(3)
  (Remark 6.4, no longer used in any proof), the reformulation of the spectral gap through the
  spectral theorem (Remark 2.9), heuristics about asymptotics, and all numerical statements.
- **How far Theorem 6.11 extends is only partly settled.** Conjugation invariance and positive
  semidefinite symmetric part imply gap = 1 − E[tr R]/n on SO(3) (Thm 6.11, machine-checked) and
  not on SO(4) (Thm 7.2, machine-checked). The prose after Theorem 7.2 shows on paper that the
  implication fails for every even n, and that for odd n the two-by-two minors cannot give a
  counterexample. For odd n ≥ 5 the question is open: `numerics/check.py` (section 9) finds no
  representation whose character ratio exceeds the standard one where sym R ⪰ 0, for n = 5, 7, 9.

## Checking it

The Lean project pins `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.0-rc2` and the Mathlib revision of the same name
(`lean/lean-toolchain`, `lean/lakefile.toml`, `lean/lake-manifest.json`). It needs
[elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan); the commands below are run from this folder.

```sh
export PATH="$HOME/.elan/bin:$PATH"                   # if `lake` is not already on the PATH
cd lean
lake exe cache get                                    # download the prebuilt Mathlib
lake build                                            # 3,584 jobs; about 80 s on a laptop once Mathlib is in place
lake env lean audit/Axioms.lean > audit/axioms.txt    # one `#print axioms` per cited declaration
cd .. && python3 tools/alignment.py --check-axioms    # names exist, axioms are the standard three
python3 numerics/check.py                             # about 30 s, needs numpy
```

`tools/alignment.py` reads the `\lean{...}` tag printed after each statement of `paper/main.tex`,
so the paper is the single source of the mapping: it fails if a tagged name is not declared in
`lean/SOnMCMC`, if a numbered result has neither a tag nor an explicit `\notformal` marker, or
if any cited declaration uses an axiom other than `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. It
prints the numbered results that are marked as not formalized (currently Theorem 7.5 and
Corollary 7.6).

To rebuild the PDF: `tectonic paper/main.tex` (or `latexmk -pdf paper/main.tex`). The PDF here
was built with Tectonic 0.17.0.

This folder was developed as `papers/son-mcmc` inside a repository that contains an auditing
tool, `proofaudit`. From the root of that repository, its deterministic stages run on this paper
and its formalization with:

```sh
.venv/bin/proofaudit run son-mcmc --config papers/son-mcmc/proofaudit-target.yaml --no-model
```

## Where the formal statements differ in form from the paper

- L² functions are functions with `MemLp f 2 m`, not equivalence classes. The Markov operator is
  a Bochner integral defined at every point.
- "The spectral gap is γ" is `IsSpectralGap m ν γ`: γ is the greatest constant in the Poincaré
  inequality. No `sSup` of a possibly empty or unbounded set is taken anywhere.
- Theorem 5.11 takes the tangent proposal `A` and the group step `R` as random variables on an
  arbitrary probability space and assumes what the paper's Definition 5.4 says (`hstep`), plus
  conjugation invariance of the law of `R` (`hν`). Theorem 5.16 discharges these hypotheses for
  the Gaussian sampler, which is `gaussSOStep n v` with `v = σ²`.
- SU(2) is `Matrix.specialUnitaryGroup (Fin 2) ℂ`; indices start at 0. The eigenspaces are
  `harm k`, a `Submodule` of functions, and their eigenvalue is `su2Eig ν k`.
- The SO(3) theorem is proved for continuous functions first; the passage to L² is made once, in
  `so3_l2ContractionBound`.
- Proposition 2.10(c) (geometric convergence of iterates) is formalized for groups written
  additively only.
- Lemma 7.3's combination of cosines is `cosSum w μ`, indexed by any finite type, with its partial
  derivative `cosSumDeriv` characterized by `hasDerivAt_cosSum_update`. In Lemma 7.4 the
  4-dimensional block is the explicit matrix `pairBlock a b` (identified with the map
  M ↦ (R_a M + M R_bᵀ)/2 by `pairBlock_mulVec`), the parity of n is a Boolean, and the density
  statements hold for every variance v ≥ 0.
